STATUTE

Courtesy translation. The Italian version is the only legally binding text.

Name and Registered Office

An association is hereby established under the name "Associazione per la REStituzione CONsapevole del Debito Ambientale - RESCONDA" (Association for the Conscious Repayment of Environmental Debt). Its registered office is in Turin, at Strada dei Tetti Bertoglio 79. Any transfer of the registered office or establishment of a secondary office shall not require an amendment to the Statute.

Duration

The association is established for an unlimited period and may be dissolved by resolution of the Extraordinary General Meeting.

Purpose – Aims

The association is non-profit and operates without discrimination on grounds of nationality, politics, religion or gender;

The objective of the association is to devise and promote initiatives aimed at:

  1. raising collective awareness of the choices that serve to restore the balance between human activities and the environment;
  2. enabling people to better assess the effectiveness and merit of proposals and initiatives aimed at improving environmental conditions and conserving ecosystems;
  3. Promoting a socially sustainable transition towards an economic model aimed at conserving natural resources and in balance with the ecosystem's capacity to regenerate itself.
  4. In general, undertaking any action aimed at restoring the balance between human activities and the environment.

The association intends to act as a qualified interlocutor for the development of information and awareness-raising activities and tools on environmental issues, in the search for a new social and economic model in which the creation and development of goods and services is determined by the natural resources available and necessary for that purpose (including the capacity to dispose of such goods and services at the end of their life cycle), as set out by the Club of Rome in its documents on the limits to growth.

To this end, the association carries out the following activities:

  • Preparation and dissemination of information material;
  • Conception, promotion, organisation and management of projects for the research, analysis, definition and implementation of knowledge tools aimed at improving the balance between humankind and the environment;
  • promotion and organisation of conventions, debates, internships, conferences, competitions, awards, etc.;
  • promotion and organisation of information, training and advanced training courses
  • preparation, publication and dissemination of editorial products, websites and web platforms, blogs and multimedia materials, periodicals and other content.
  • Training of experts capable of developing the necessary tools and actions;
  • Advising decision makers in order to enable them to operate more effectively;
  • Participation in bodies, including political or institutional ones, whose action has environmental implications.
  • Participation in conventions, seminars, courses and events, including international ones
  • Collaboration with national and international media;
  • Participation in, and organisation and promotion of - in collaboration with other bodies – cultural events consistent with the association's purpose.
  • Access, where necessary, to public or private funding, providing the required guarantees.
  • anything else conducive to achieving the association's purpose, such as entering into agreements with public or private bodies, providing the required guarantees;

In pursuing its aims, the Association may hire or engage experts, technicians, artists, lecturers or other specialised personnel from outside the Association.

The above activities are carried out by the Association mainly through the services provided by its members.

Composition of the Association

Members are divided into the following categories:

  1. Founding Members: those who established the association by signing the deed of incorporation.
  2. Ordinary Members: natural persons, legal persons or bodies whose application for admission has been accepted by the Board of Directors and who pay, upon admission, the established membership fee.
  3. Supporting Members: members who contribute through particular financial contributions. The minimum contribution required to obtain and retain the status of Supporting Member is determined by the General Meeting.

The division of Members into the above categories does not imply any difference in treatment with regard to their rights and duties towards the Association.

Admission criteria

To obtain the status of Member, each applicant must submit a signed application on the appropriate form, delivering it in person or sending it by post and/or e-mail to the Board of Directors.

If no notice of rejection of the application is given by the Board of Directors within 90 days of the date of its submission, the application shall be deemed accepted.

The Board of Directors may propose the rejection of the application to the General Meeting of Members, which decides by a 2/3 majority. Any rejection of the application must state the reasons, and the applicant may appeal against that decision to the first General Meeting convened thereafter.

Rights of Members and Supporters

A member who is up to date with the annual renewal of membership:

  • Has the right to take part in the activities promoted by the Association
  • Is part of the General Meeting and votes in it.
  • Has the right to be informed about the programmes, activities and initiatives of the Association.
  • Has the right to elect the governing bodies and to be elected to them.
  • Has the right to withdraw from the Association.

The rights of an individual member may be restricted or suspended in accordance with the procedures of this Statute

Duties of Members and Supporters

Each Member must:

  1. Accept and comply with the rules contained in the Deed of Incorporation, in the Statute and in all resolutions of the General Meeting of Members and/or of the Board of Directors;
  2. Avoid any conduct that may bring the Association into disrepute;
  3. pay the membership fee set annually, by the deadline set by the Board of Directors;
  4. cooperate in achieving the aims of the association.
  5. behave fairly and transparently, respecting the dignity and opinions of all.
  6. refer the resolution of any internal disputes to the governing bodies.
  7. Members called upon to hold offices and roles must do so with diligence and responsibility.

Loss of the status of Member or Supporter

The status of “Member” is lost through death, withdrawal, default in payment or expulsion. Default in payment is declared by the Board of Directors.

Resignation as a member must be made in writing, delivered in person or sent by post and/or e-mail to the Board of Directors, and takes effect immediately upon receipt.

Expulsion is resolved by the Board of Directors against a member or supporter:

  • who fails to comply with the provisions of this Statute and the resolutions adopted by the bodies of the Association;
  • who, in any way, causes or may cause serious harm, including moral harm, to the Association.

The decision of the Board of Directors must subsequently be ratified by the first ordinary General Meeting of Members. During that meeting, to which the member concerned must be summoned, the charges are examined in an adversarial hearing with the member concerned.

Expulsion must be notified to the expelled member and becomes effective upon its entry in the register of members.

Failure to pay the annual membership fee within two months from the start of the financial year results in the automatic forfeiture of membership without the need for any formality.

Members who have withdrawn, forfeited membership or been expelled are not entitled to reimbursement of the membership contribution paid.

Resources of the Association

The economic and financial resources for the operation of the Association derive from:

  1. fees and contributions from members;
  2. contributions from private individuals;
  3. contributions from Institutions or public bodies or local, national or international organisations;
  4. assets that become the property of the Association;
  5. any reserve funds established from budget surpluses;
  6. any grants, inheritances, donations and legacies.
  7. income from promotional initiatives aimed at its own funding;
  8. other income compatible with the aims of the association.

These resources contribute to forming the common fund, which may never be divided among the members during the life of the association or upon its dissolution. The distribution, even indirectly or on a deferred basis, of profits or operating surpluses, as well as of funds, reserves or capital, is prohibited, unless such allocation or distribution is required by law. The Association is obliged to reinvest any operating surplus in the institutional activities provided for in the Statute.

Membership fee

The amount of the membership fee is set annually by the Board of Directors, approved by the General Meeting of Members and communicated in writing to the Members themselves; The membership fee is non-transferable.

Financial year

The financial year ends on 31 December of each year.

The Board of Directors must prepare the financial statements or income and expenditure report, which must be approved by the General Meeting of Members within four months of the end of the financial year.

Bodies of the Association

The bodies of the Association are the General Meeting of Members and the Board of Directors.

If necessary, the General Meeting shall appoint an auditing body or, in general, any other executive or operational Body useful for the pursuit of the institutional aims.

The General Meeting of Members

The General Meeting of Members is the highest decision-making body of the Association; it consists of all Members who are up to date with payment of the membership fee, and the Board of Directors is responsible for implementing its decisions.

Convening of the General Meeting of Members

Members are convened to the General Meeting by the President at least once a year by 30 April, by means of a notice of meeting containing the agenda.

The General Meeting meets in extraordinary session whenever the President deems it necessary or when a reasoned and signed request is made by at least two members of the Board of Directors or one tenth (1/10) of the Members. In the latter cases, should the President fail to convene the meeting within thirty days of the request, it may be convened within a further thirty days by the Vice-President and, should the latter fail to do so, it may be requested from the President of the Court of Turin.

The General Meeting is convened without any formal requirements, provided that verifiable means are used, including e-mail and/or publication on the official website of the Association.

Notice of the meeting must be given at least ten days before the date set for the meeting

Meetings of the General Meeting may also be held by audio-video conference, provided that this is stated in the notice of meeting and that Members are provided, no later than the day before the General Meeting, with the information necessary to participate remotely.

At first call, the General Meeting is validly constituted when at least half plus one of the members entitled to vote are present or represented.

At second call, the General Meeting is validly constituted whatever the number of members entitled to vote who are present or represented.

Chairing of the General Meeting

The General Meeting is chaired by the President or, in the event of the President's absence or inability to act, by the Vice-President. In the event of absence or inability to act of both the President and the Vice-President, the General Meeting is chaired by the eldest member of the Board of Directors present.

The Chair of the General Meeting has full powers to conduct the General Meeting. The Chair verifies the right of those present to attend the General Meeting, in order to ascertain whether it is validly constituted and whether the quorum required for passing resolutions is met. The Chair of the General Meeting is responsible for verifying the right to attend and for conducting the discussion and the vote, setting the procedures for them, unless otherwise provided by Law.

Resolutions of the General Meeting

The General Meeting passes resolutions by an absolute majority of its members (50%+1) at first call, and by a relative majority at second call.

Amendments to the Statute must be approved by the favourable vote of at least two thirds of those taking part in the vote.

A resolution to dissolve the Association and to devolve its assets requires the favourable vote of at least three quarters of the members.

The General Meeting votes by open ballot. Resolutions adopted by the General Meeting must be minuted by a minute-taking secretary appointed by the General Meeting on the proposal of the President.

Directors have no vote on resolutions concerning their own liability.

Voting by proxy is not permitted.

Powers of the General Meeting of Members

The General Meeting of Members resolves on all matters relating to the management of the Association reserved to its competence by this Statute and on any proposal submitted to it, provided that it does not fall within the competence of the Extraordinary General Meeting.

In particular, the duties of the General Meeting are to:

  1. elect the members of the Board of Directors
  2. approve the annual financial statements
  3. set out for the Board of Directors the general guidelines for planning activities
  4. ratify the proposals of the Board of Directors
  5. resolve on the rejection of applications and on the expulsion of Members referred to in Article 8

The Board of Directors

The Association is managed by a Board of Directors consisting of a minimum of three and a maximum of five Directors, elected by the General Meeting of Members for a term of three years. The number of members is determined by the General Meeting.

In the event of the resignation, expulsion or death of a Director, the Board replaces them by appointing the first of the candidates not elected, who remains in office until the expiry of the term of the entire Board, subject to ratification by the next General Meeting of Members.

Convening of the Board of Directors

The Board of Directors meets when convened by the President, when the President or at least two Directors so request in writing, including by e-mail.

The meeting is convened by the President by means of a notice stating the date, time and place of the meeting, as well as the agenda, to be sent without any formal requirements, provided that verifiable means are used, including e-mail, at least seven days before the date set for the meeting, except in urgent cases, in which the meeting may be convened in the same manner at least two days before the meeting.

Functioning of the Board of Directors

The Board of Directors is chaired by the President of the Association or, in the event of the President's absence or inability to act, by the Vice-President or, in the event of the latter's absence or inability to act, by the eldest member of the Board of Directors present.

Meetings may be held by videoconference, provided that all participants can be identified.

Proposed Resolutions, unless otherwise specified, are approved by a simple majority of those present. In the event of a tie, the vote of the President, or of whoever acts in the President's stead, counts double.

Resolutions are adopted by open ballot. Voting by proxy is not permitted.

Members of the Board must abstain from voting on matters concerning themselves personally.

A meeting of the Board is valid if a majority of the Directors is present.

Minutes are drawn up for meetings of the Board and are signed by the President and the Secretary.

Powers of the Board of Directors

The Board is vested with the broadest powers for the ordinary and extraordinary management of the Association.

Accordingly, by way of example, the Board is responsible for:

  1. implementing the resolutions of the General Meeting;
  2. drawing up the financial statements or income and expenditure report;
  3. drawing up programmes of the activities to be carried out
  4. drawing up plans for the use of operating surpluses
  5. preparing any internal regulations;
  6. entering into deeds and contracts relating to the activities of the Association;
  7. resolving on the withdrawal and expulsion of members;
  8. appointing employees and staff and determining their remuneration;
  9. performing all acts and operations for the proper administration of the Association that do not fall to the General Meeting of Members, including setting the annual membership fee;
  10. overseeing the proper functioning of all the Association's activities and coordinating them.

The legal representation of the Association before third parties and in legal proceedings is vested in the President of the Board of Directors. The President may, on a case-by-case or permanent basis – provided this is done in writing - confer legal representation on one or more Directors, including severally.

Each Director, severally, attends to the implementation of the resolutions of the General Meeting and of the Board of Directors.

In urgent cases, each Director, severally, may exercise the powers of the Board of Directors, subject to ratification by the Board at its next meeting.

Powers of the President of the Association

The President holds the legal and representative powers of the Association before third parties and in legal proceedings

The President directs and coordinates, in general, all the activities of the Association, except for matters expressly

reserved to other Bodies; the President is the guarantor of the Statute and oversees the implementation of the resolutions of the General Meeting and of the Board of Directors.

The President is responsible for:

  1. exercising all powers of initiative necessary for the proper functioning of the Association;
  1. overseeing the implementation of the programme;
  2. convening and chairing the meetings of the General Meeting and of the Board of Directors, setting the agenda and conducting proceedings;
  3. coordinating communication activities and giving visibility to the activities of the Association;
  4. referring any member for disciplinary reasons, including by adopting urgent and immediate measures.
  5. may grant specific delegations of powers to members of the Board of Directors for the exercise of the President's powers.
  6. in agreement with the Board of Directors, conferring special delegations of powers, including on persons from outside the Association, for the performance of particular activities.
  7. adopting urgent measures, with the obligation to report on them to the Board of Directors within 30 days so that it may ratify the presidential decisions adopted as a matter of urgency.

Vice-President

The Board of Directors may appoint, on the proposal of the President, a Vice-President from among its members. The Vice-President assists the President in their activities and temporarily replaces the President in the event of absence, inability to act or resignation, until the election and proclamation of the new President.

Secretary

The Board of Directors appoints a Secretary from among its members. The Secretary is responsible for drawing up the minutes, which the Secretary transcribes into the appropriate books entrusted to the Secretary's custody.

The Secretary also coordinates, in accordance with the directions of the President and of the Board of Directors, the activities of the Association, acting as a point of reference during the implementation of its programmes.

The Secretary remains in office for a period coinciding with the term of office of the Board of Directors.

Publicity and transparency of the Association's records

Substantial publicity and transparency are ensured for records relating to the activities of the Association, with particular reference to the annual Financial Statements or Reports.

These records, kept at the registered office, must be made available to members for consultation; anyone wishing to obtain a copy of the documents shall bear the related costs.

Dissolution

The dissolution of the Association must be resolved by the General Meeting with the favourable vote of at least three quarters of the members entitled to vote. In the event of the dissolution of the Association, a liquidator shall be appointed, who may also be a non-member, to handle the liquidation of all assets and discharge the outstanding obligations.

All remaining assets shall be devolved to other Associations pursuing similar aims or to purposes of public benefit, unless a different allocation is required by law.

Arbitration clause and reference to the law.

Any dispute that may arise between the members - or their successors in title - and the Association shall be referred to a sole arbitrator appointed by the parties or, failing agreement, by the Turin Chamber of Commerce (CCIAA). The arbitrator shall decide ex bono et aequo, without procedural constraints, and the decision shall be final and not subject to appeal. For all matters not expressly provided for herein, reference is made to the laws in force.
